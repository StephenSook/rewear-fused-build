01 The thirty-second version

REWEAR-FUSED is one computational co-design loop that designs two matched molecules at once to close the textile-to-textile recycling loop on children's stretch apparel, non-recyclable today because of its elastane.

Instead of attacking existing elastane (crystalline, hostile, it defeats every native enzyme on the intact fiber), we invert the problem. Engine A, your lane, screens a new, enzymatically-cleavable elastane fiber. Engine B, your lane, designs the matched carbamate hydrolase built to cut that exact bond. Engine C is the live Digital Recyclability Passport.

"Carter's, here's the molecule for your next onesie, and the matched enzyme that closes its loop. No one has co-designed the pair, together, for textiles."

10 Your Q&A

Did you actually design a de-novo enzyme?
Honestly, not yet, and we never claim it. We ship three LigandMPNN redesigns of real serine-hydrolase scaffolds (IsPETase 6EQE TM 0.82, FAST-PETase 7SH6 TM 0.79, CalB 1TCA TM 0.85), in-silico. The de-novo fold (TM<0.5, no prior art) is the deferred GPU swing. We built the full pipeline and a swap script that refuses TM≥0.5 so we cannot overclaim. The redesign is real; an invented de-novo would have been the loss condition.
What structure am I looking at and downloading?
The CalB / PDB 1TCA serine-hydrolase reference scaffold, the real template enzyme-003 is a LigandMPNN redesign of. It is labeled as the reference, not an expressed protein. The file is the authentic 1TCA crystal; the active-site highlight is its Ser105-Asp187-His224 triad.
Are your PLACER numbers measured?
No, they are in-silico design targets on the Lauko (Science 2025) thresholds, reported with an uncertainty band. The lead shows preorg 0.74 +/- 0.03, pLDDT 90.1 (active-site 95.2), Ca-RMSD 0.38, scRMSD 1.18. The UI says "Design metrics, not an expressed protein." We ship no kcat/KM because urethane hydrolysis is harder than the ester chemistry those numbers come from.
Can AI really invent a new block copolymer like elastane?
We never claim that. Engine A is constraint-guided virtual screening: enumerate 612 from known aliphatic isocyanates (HDI/IPDI/H12MDI), soft diols (PCL/polycarbonate ~2000 g/mol), chain extenders (BDO/EDA), score 487, filter 23, surface 10. Polymer LMs only handle homopolymers and random copolymers, not segmented block PU-ureas.
Doesn't your enzyme need pH 10, which destroys the cotton?
That's the wild-type UMG-SP2 problem (Ser-Ser-cis-Lys, pH ~10). We anchor on its transition-state geometry, not its protein, and design a Ser-His-Asp triad near-neutral. All three designs are Ser-His-Asp, pH 7.0-8.5; the validator rejects anything outside that band.
Won't making it cleavable destroy the stretch?
No. The cleavable carbamate is in the soft amorphous segment, not the crystalline load-bearing domains. All 10 candidates sit in the 25-35 wt% window, elongation 440-620%, soft Tg below -50. The trade-off curve shows the window with "<17 too fast, >40 won't degrade", cited to Xu 2026 and Qin 2024.
What toxic products come off when you cleave it?
None of concern. Aliphatic isocyanates only, which cleave to aliphatic diamines, not aromatic MDA/TDA. The validator rejects non-aliphatic isocyanates; the passport shows "aromatic amine release: NONE".
How do I know these numbers were not just typed in?
Every artifact is SHA-256 signed in the manifest with producedBy provenance, and validate_artifacts.py --strict rejects a stale manifest on any byte change. We disclose the limit honestly: the manifest records a model version only for Engine C; there is no committed GPU-pipeline version for A/B because that heavy run is the deferred swing. We label the redesigns and design-target metrics as in-silico rather than dress them as committed outputs.
