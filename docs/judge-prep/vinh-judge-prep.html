01 The thirty-second version

REWEAR-FUSED is one computational co-design loop that designs two matched molecules at once to close the textile-to-textile recycling loop on children's stretch apparel, which is non-recyclable today because of its elastane.

Instead of attacking existing elastane (crystalline, hostile, it defeats every native enzyme on the intact fiber), we invert the problem. Engine A screens a new, enzymatically-cleavable elastane fiber. Engine B designs the matched carbamate hydrolase built to cut that exact bond. Engine C, your lane, is the live Digital Recyclability Passport that proves a garment is safe and routes it.

"Carter's, here's the molecule for your next onesie, and the matched enzyme that closes its loop. No one has co-designed the pair, together, for textiles."
 GARMENT ──▶ ENGINE C  passport: clear / lab-test / divert      ◀── your lane
            └─▶ ENGINE A  cleavable elastane  (constraint-guided screening)
                  └─[carbamate substrate target]─▶ ENGINE B  matched hydrolase
                        └─ cleaves bond ─▶ recoverable monomers ─▶ NEW onesie

02 The two walls (why this exists)

Carter's collects worn baby clothes through KIDCYCLE / TerraCycle, then shreds them into pet bedding. Two walls stop a onesie becoming a onesie again:

  • Wall 1, chemical safety. Nobody can prove the recovered fabric is chemically safe for infant skin. This is the wall Engine C knocks down.
  • Wall 2, elastane. Nobody can break down the elastane in every stretch garment. (Engines A + B.)
Lead with this

Elastane is in ~80% of all clothing, and as little as ~1% of it makes a recycler reject the whole garment. (Fashion for Good, 2026.)

Scale

~92M tonnes of textile waste a year (UNEP); only ~1% recycled fiber-to-fiber today.

04 The de-novo question (the one trap to never fall in)

Honesty guardrail · everyone says this the same way

The shipped enzymes are LigandMPNN redesigns of real serine-hydrolase scaffolds (TM 0.79 to 0.85), not de-novo folds. The de-novo fold (TM < 0.5, no prior art) is the GPU run we deferred. The "shouldn't be possible" novelty is the co-design loop itself, which is true and runs live. (This is Pravin's lane; you just need to never call the enzyme de-novo.)

06 The eight judge-defense answers (universal)

What toxic products come off when you cleave it?
None of concern. Aliphatic isocyanates only, so cleavage gives aliphatic diamines, not aromatic MDA/TDA carcinogens. The passport shows a green "aromatic amine release: NONE".
Doesn't your enzyme need pH 10, which destroys the cotton?
That's the wild-type trap. We design a Ser-His-Asp triad that works near-neutral, pH 7.0-8.5, cotton-compatible.
Won't making it cleavable destroy the stretch?
No. The cleavable bond lives in the soft amorphous segment, not the load-bearing crystalline domains. 25-35 wt% window, 440-620% elongation.
Can AI really invent a new block copolymer?
We never claim that. Engine A is constraint-guided screening: enumerate 612, score 487, filter 23, surface 10.
Did you actually make any of this in a lab?
No, entirely in-silico for the molecules (Engine A TRL 3, Engine B TRL 2). Engine C, my lane, is TRL 6 and runs live.
What's the business model?
We replace the TerraCycle downcycle line item with a fiber-to-fiber loop plus an SB 707 / EU-DPP compliance asset. We're the Nexus Circular of textiles.
Isn't this two projects?
One inverse-design loop. The carbamate bond in the fiber is the exact bond the enzyme cuts; topPairs.json cross-references each enzyme to its matched fiber.
Will Carter's pay for this?
They already pay TerraCycle to downcycle. Engine C lowers their SB 707 eco-modulated EPR fees and pre-positions EU DPP. Same spend, better outcome, deployable in Year 1.

09 Your lane · Engine C, the Digital Recyclability Passport

A hybrid neuro-symbolic compliance system. A deterministic regulatory rule layer runs first; a CatBoost text classifier colors the residual rare-hazard categories. It routes each garment clear / lab-test / divert and emits a cryptographically signed Digital Product Passport. It is deployed, live, and trained on real public data.

 GARMENT ─▶ [1] deterministic rules (cite the statute)
              FAIL → divert    FLAG → lab-test    else → clear
         ─▶ [2] CatBoost text model colors rare residual hazards (>0.5 → lab-test)
         ─▶ [3] sign W3C VC 2.0 passport (Ed25519) + GS1 Digital Link QR
 served by FastAPI on Render; frozen-parity self-check refuses to boot on drift

The real data (say the number with confidence)

  • 631 unique real CPSC SaferProducts recalls, pulled live from saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall across 15 apparel/children-textile query terms, deduped by RecallID, saved to cpsc_recalls.parquet. Corpus spans 1974 to 2026-06-11. No synthetic rows (we explicitly rejected the spec's synthetic-SKU suggestion, the APEX lesson).
  • 3 trainable labels with real positive counts: flammability/sleepwear n=206, drawstring n=142, lead substrate n=12.

The model + the metric honesty

  • CatBoost text classifier, version catboost-text-v1.0 (iterations 350, depth 4, lr 0.08, Logloss), out-of-fold 5-fold StratifiedKFold.
  • We report PR-AUC, not ROC-AUC, because positives are a minority (<5% per label; lead has only 12). ROC is misleadingly high on rare positives. Frozen macro: PR-AUC 0.732, ROC-AUC 0.951.
  • Anti-leakage: we strip the label-defining keywords (lead, phthalate, flammable, sleepwear, drawstring, pfas) from the feature text before training, so the model must predict from genuine correlates, not relearn the labeling keyword and report a meaningless ~1.0.

13 Your honesty guardrails (Engine C specifics)

  • Not "85+ thresholds enforced". Three deterministic rules are wired today (ESPR elastane, AB 1817 PFAS, REACH Entry 43 amines) over a 7-regulation catalog. The README says "~20% of the spec / reference implementation". 85+ is the roadmap target.
  • Not "trained on CPSC + EU Safety Gate + PFAS + OEKO-TEX". Only CPSC (631 recalls) is wired. DevPost lists the others; reconcile to "CPSC is the wired source today; EU Safety Gate, the Peaslee PFAS set, and OEKO-TEX negatives are named in our roadmap."
  • Not "PR-AUC 0.85". That is the handoff target. The real frozen macro is 0.732. Report the achieved number.
  • compositionDrivers is NOT SHAP. They are real composition ratios (elastane %, synthetic %, archetype flag), deliberately not dressed as model explanations. SHAP is roadmap.
  • The Render API's /judges route 404s on the live deploy (it predates that commit). Don't promise "hit /judges on the API". recallCount=631 is verifiable via the manifest + /healthz. (The frontend /judges page on Vercel is fine.)
  • Parity is OUTPUT, not model-binary. Say "output parity, byte-identical served outputs", not "the model binary is hash-locked".

14 Your Q&A

Is this trained on real data or did you fake the dataset?
Real. 631 unique CPSC SaferProducts recalls pulled live from saferproducts.gov, saved to a parquet, count verified. No synthetic rows. The spec suggested a synthetic 10,000-SKU population; we explicitly rejected it to avoid the synthetic-data failure mode.
Why PR-AUC instead of accuracy or ROC-AUC?
Positives are a minority (<5% per label; lead has 12 in 631). ROC is misleadingly high there, so we report PR-AUC (average precision) out-of-fold via 5-fold CV. Macro 0.732 / ROC 0.951. The slow test asserts flammability PR-AUC > 0.7 and lead PR-AUC < 0.8, since lead is rare and honestly hard from text.
How do I know the model didn't memorize the labeling keyword?
model.py strips the label-defining terms from the feature text before training. With them gone the model must predict the hazard from product type, brand, and co-occurring language. Without the strip it would relearn the keyword and report a meaningless ~1.0.
Is the live endpoint serving the same model that made your artifacts?
Yes, enforced by hash. _verify_frozen_parity() hashes every manifest file at import and refuses to boot on mismatch. /healthz returns frozen_parity_sha bb95f4e8 and output_sha_ok true; /classify byte-matches the artifact. It is output parity by design, not a model-binary hash.
Is the passport signature real or a placeholder?
Real Ed25519. build.py signs the canonical JSON of the credential with an Ed25519 private key, producing the base64 signatureValue. The issuer is a spec-compliant did:key. The frontend verifies it in-browser, no server, with a live tamper toggle.
How many regulations does it really enforce?
Three deterministic rules wired today over a 7-regulation catalog, each citing its real statute. The full target is 85+ thresholds; the README labels the shipped state ~20% of the spec. What is wired is real and cited; the rest is roadmap.
Will the demo break if Render is down?
No. The frontend renders the passport view entirely off the static signed artifacts. The live endpoint proves it is a real deployed service; the static bundle guarantees the demo runs offline. A keepalive cron keeps the free-tier service warm.
